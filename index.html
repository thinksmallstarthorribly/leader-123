HE ASKED UNTIL I LOST.

Sixteen years ago. Wrong shoes. Two pages of excuses. One conversation that still runs this programme.

I wore the wrong shoes to work. Jason, my team leader, was sent to talk to me about the dress code. I came back to the follow-up with two pages of notes on what everyone else was wearing. I was certain I was being singled out.

He never raised his voice. He asked questions until I talked myself into the corner.

Jason
"So you feel that because other people are doing the wrong thing, you should be able to do the wrong thing too?"

Fuck. 2-0 and finished.

Monday I wore the right clothes. He said he was proud of me. One sentence. I come from a background where that was not something I heard often. It held a lot of weight.

What I did not know: management was already scouting me. Jason knew. He was not managing a dress code. He was leading me toward something I could not see yet.

I have told that story to close to 3,000 people. Ask, don't tell. Recognition as currency. Identity drives behaviour. Leading from competency, not the title. All of it was in that one conversation.

Then they gave me a team. Sixteen people. I had the title and none of the capability. Peter Principle, live: risen to my highest level of incompetence. People showed up every day with problems. I thought if I got involved in every one of those lives I would collapse, so I went impersonal. I'm the boss. Not: how can I serve you.

I slipped around in my own shit for a while.

Then someone finally taught me frameworks. It opened a part of my brain that still runs everything. I asked to move into learning and development, got serious about it, then asked to go back into management with a new team. It came naturally. I was the first Manager of the Quarter. Name on a glass plaque in the foyer. I did not see it coming. The team I had kept at arm's length took me out for my birthday.

The rule I had to beat out of myself: shut up and listen. I was good at telling. I did not want a personal relationship with someone else's problems. That was the skill. I was dodging it.

"I made all the mistakes in the guide. That's how I know them so well. And that's why I wrote them down."

— Alex Cooper, Creator of Leader 1-2-3
